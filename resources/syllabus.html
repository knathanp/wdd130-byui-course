WDD 130: Web Fundamentals Syllabus

Welcome to WDD 130! This class serves as an introduction to web design and development. The course is designed to introduce students to basics of web technology, web design, and web development principles. The course uses hands-on activities with students participating in simple web design projects and programming. It is anticipated that students who complete this course will understand the fields of web design and development and will have a good idea of whether or not they want to pursue web design and development as a career.

Time Consumption

This is a two-credit course, plan to spend approximately 4-6 hours each week doing the homework.

Text Book

There is no textbook for this course. All the readings and activities will be online.

Course Objectives

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

  • Demonstrate basic proficiency in using current, valid, and semantic Hypertext Markup Language (HTML) syntax to define the structure and content of a webpage.
  • Demonstrate basic proficiency in using current valid Cascading Style Sheets (CSS) to style an HTML document.
  • Plan, design, and develop web pages and sites according to best practices of organization and maintainability.
  • Discover and analyze the web design and development industry as a career path.
  • Work effectively with others by communicating clearly, collaborating as a team member, fulfilling assignments and meeting deadlines.

Growth Learning - Acorn Stages

Allow the seeds to be planted (Weeks 1-4)

Use your agency to allow the acorn to be planted in the soil of you minds and hearts. You will receive information from observing the teacher, watching videos, reading reference, and studying the materials in your minds.

You be given all the content about HTML, CSS, and web page design necessary to complete all the assignments for the semester.

Noursish and cultivate the seeds until they begin to sprout (Weeks 5-9)

Act on your acorn by nourishing it with the personal revelation that comes through trial, failure, reflection, and improvement. You will plan, design, and create individually. You will practice implementing the information that was planted. The teacher will be close by for support in this critical stage. The actions you take will sprout roots as a foundation of your understanding and skills.

You will be assigned challenges in class to practice using all the content you were given in the first stage.

Branch out and grow together (Weeks 10-13)

You will start to grow up and branch out to mentor and strengthen each other. You will reach higher, spread your branches to support each other, plant seeds for others, and grow ever stronger roots as you continue to improve. You will plan, design, and create as collaborative groups. You will teach and support each other and strengthen your understanding and abilities. The teacher will cheer you on. The world will see your work and it will be amazing.

You will be given a collaborative final project where you are a client, a lead developer, and an assistant developer to multiple sites. These sites will be managed online and published online.

Late work

You are encouraged to turn in all work on time, but I understand that sometimes there are circumstances beyond your control. For that reason, I will accept late assignments, but there will be an automatic deduction of 2% per day, down to a maximum of 20% off (so you can receive up to 80% no matter how late the assignment). You are encouraged to turn in all assignments, even if they are late and receive only minimal credit. Because of the time needed to grade the final project before grades must be submitted, the final project will not be accepted late.

Why can’t you submit assignments for full credit when they’re late? The deadlines actually help you. First, enforcing deadlines helps you stay on track and not get behind, which is what can happen if there are no firm deadlines. (That’s definitely what happens for me without deadlines.) Second, for many assignments, I share a sample solution after the due date has passed. It would not be fair to give credit for assignments turned in after the solution has been made available. Third, enforcing deadlines helps you develop the kind of responsibility that will be a blessing to you in employment and many other things.

Extenuating circumstances should be discussed with me prior to the assignment due date, however, in most cases, the late penalty will still apply.

Generative AI Use

The use of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in this course is not forbidden. But the majority of your use of AI in this course is to use it as a tutor, not to write code for you. Unless specifically instructed, all projects that are turned in for points in the course should be your own code and not copying code given to you by AI. Read the CSE Department Generative AI Usage Policy. For this class, you are encouraged to use Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT to answer your questions about HTML, CSS, and web development. You SHOULD NOT use Generative AI tools to create CSS or HTML for you.

Honor Code

ACADEMIC HONESTY

You are expected to follow the university's policies for academic honesty.

You may work with your classmates but all submitted work for projects and assignments must be original. Share ideas; do not share code! Assistance from a classmate should be on par with the help you would expect from a lab assistant. You should not simply copy/paste from another student.

If you work closely with another student, helping teach and learn from each other, make sure you each still write your own code, but in this case, your solutions may end up being very similar. This is completely fine, but please make sure to put a comment in your code stating that you wrote your own program, but worked closely with that person, and that is why it is similar.

You are encouraged to use the Internet as a resource, but recognize that you should not copy and paste someone else’s work as your own. Cite all sources and follow copyright laws. When in doubt, give credit and be upfront.

Do not look for or share solutions on "note sharing" internet sites.

The penalty for copying or plagiarism of assignments might be one or more of the following: 0% on an assignment, being asked to withdraw from the class, a failing grade in the class, or disciplinary action by the University.

Please read this document on plagiarism.
